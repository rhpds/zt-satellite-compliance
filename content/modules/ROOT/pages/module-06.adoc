== Create a compliance policy

Navigate to **Hosts > Compliance > Policies** in the Satellite Web UI and click **New Policy**.

== Configure the compliance policy

.Deployment Options tab
[%collapsible]
=====
In the `Deployment Options` tab, select the following:

. Select `Ansible`.
. Click `Next`.

.Deployment Options tab
image::policies-deployment-options.png[]
=====

.Policy Attributes tab
[%collapsible]
=====
In the `Policy Attributes` tab, enter the following information:

. In the `Name` field, enter `DISA STIG no empty passwords ssh`.
. Click `Next`.

.Policy Attributes tab
image::policies-policy-attributes.png[]
=====

.SCAP Content tab
[%collapsible]
=====
In the `SCAP Content` tab, select the following:

. In the `SCAP Content` field, select `Red Hat RHEL10 default content`.
. In the `XCCDF Profile` field, select `Red Hat STIG for Red Hat Enterprise Linux 10`.
. In the `Tailoring File` field, select `STIG SSHD Tailoring file`.
. In the `XCCDF Profile in Tailoring File` field, select `DISA STIG for RHEL10 No Empty Passwords`.
. Click `Next`.

[NOTE]
====
*Red Hat STIG vs DISA STIG*

Red Hat STIG is authored by Red Hat and provides STIG-style hardening based on DISA's requirements, but tailored to how RHEL actually works. It's available immediately in the scap-security-guide package and is what you see in Satellite as profiles like `xccdf_org.ssgproject.content_profile_stig`.

DISA STIG is the official benchmark published by the Defense Information Systems Agency. It goes through a formal approval process and is published on DISA's STIG website. It's the authoritative source required for DoD compliance.

Key differences:

- *Timing* — Red Hat's STIG is available at or near OS release; DISA's official STIG can lag months behind while going through the approval process.
- *Authority* — For DoD/government compliance audits, the DISA STIG is the one auditors check against. Red Hat's version is a close approximation but not the officially signed-off document.
- *Content* — They're largely aligned since Red Hat works with DISA during development, but there can be minor differences in rule implementation, check logic, or remediation steps.

In practice, most organizations use the Red Hat STIG profile from scap-security-guide (which is what's in your Satellite) for automated scanning, since it maps to the same controls and is kept in sync with DISA's requirements.
